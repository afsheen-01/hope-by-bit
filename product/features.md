# The product

What Hope By Bit is. Everything built follows this file; where they disagree, this file wins.

## The daily unit

The whole product fits on one screen.

**One good news story**, matched to three to five interests picked at setup: medicine, climate, wild places, kindness, science, your city, rights, food. One story, then the screen ends
([P1](principles.md)). Each story has:

- a headline under 64 characters that names the change
- a number, and what it counts
- two or three short paragraphs, under 90 words in all
- a named source

**One small thing to do with your body.** Two or three options, such as *a glass of water*, *standup and stretch* or *ninety seconds outside*. Each is free, needs no signup and takes under a minute.
Any of them adds the same entry to your wall.

Reading good news is information. Standing up and feeling the difference is experience, and that is what lasts through a bad afternoon.

**The closing line** reflects what you did: none yet is *"That's today's bit."*, one or two is
*"That's today's win."*, all three is *"All three. A big day."* It isn't saved. The wall's total is the only number the product keeps ([P2](principles.md)), because a daily score is just a streak.
Then it says when the next story comes, and the screen ends.

**No donate or join buttons here.**

## Keepsakes and the wall

**Keepsakes** are the stories you chose to keep. **The wall** is everything you did and kept, newest first, with a total that only grows.

On a bad day, you revisit your own evidence. The loop breaker shows your Keepsakes first, because a story you chose means more than one we picked.

**Your record follows you.** Signing in is optional. Without an account everything works on one device; an account brings your wall, Keepsakes and settings to every device ([P9](principles.md)).

## The two hard-moment buttons

**Break the loop, for your head.** Use it as often as you need. Sixty seconds, three steps you can
skip:

1. A breath: 4 in, 3 hold, 6 out. Shown, and optionally spoken, so it works in the dark.
2. One of your own Keepsakes, with the date you kept it: *"Three weeks ago you kept this."*
3. One sensory question: *name one thing you can hear.*

It ends with *"That's the loop broken, for now. If it closes again in ten minutes, come back."*
Your own past actions convince where general reassurance doesn't. Runs are counted on the device
and never leave it.

**Optionally, the third run tells your people.** You choose this at setup, while calm; it is off by
default. At most one message a day. Afterwards, one tap sends
*let them know I'm alright*.

The message says it was automatic: *"[Name] has been using their grounding tool a lot today. They could use a check-in from a friend today."* A hard day that stops at two runs sends nothing, which is
why the check-in button and the crisis line are always there.

**Check in, for your people.** Pick two or three people at setup and write the message then, so the hard moment asks nothing of you.

**Each person agrees first.** They get one message: *"[Name] chose you as someone to reach out to when things are heavy. Reply YES to be reachable, STOP to decline."* Only a YES makes them reachable, and the site shows who has confirmed. They only need a phone.

**Signed in, we send the messages**, which is what makes the automatic third-run message possible.
**Signed out, the site opens your phone's share sheet**, so the message goes from your own number.

## Voice

So it works with your eyes closed, in the dark, at 3am. Everything runs in the browser, on the
device.

- **Reading out:** the daily story, slower than usual, and the whole loop breaker, with the breath
  guided: *"in… hold… and out."* Long silences are on purpose.
- **Speaking to it:** a short fixed list of words: keep, skip, next, done, the sensory answers,leave. Anything else returns you to the buttons. This only turns on where the browser can recognise speech on the device.

## Vibration

Where the browser supports it, a short vibration marks an action landing on the wall and paces the
loop breaker's breath. It is never the only signal, and a setting turns it off.

## The content standard

Finding the stories is the hard part: real progress, specific numbers, things that actually happened.

Once a night, the pipeline pulls about 200 candidates and ranks them. One person reviews the top 30 in twenty minutes and schedules one story per interest, a week ahead. Sources include Our World in Data, Gavi, WHO, IEA, IUCN, nobel prize website and national statistics offices; other good news sites are leads to check, not sources.

## Tone

**Plain, specific, warm.** Write like someone glad you came who won't make a fuss about it.

Second person, present tense, everyday words. Warm doesn't mean cheerful: every line should read well to someone having the worst week of their life.
